Solar energy is one of the fastest-growing sources of renewable power in the world. It harnesses energy from the sun using photovoltaic panels or concentrated solar power systems. The cost of solar panels has dropped dramatically over the past decade, making solar energy increasingly competitive with fossil fuels. Many countries are investing heavily in solar infrastructure as part of their strategies to reduce carbon emissions and combat climate change.

Photovoltaic cells work by converting sunlight directly into electricity through the photovoltaic effect. When photons from sunlight strike the surface of a semiconductor material, they knock electrons loose, creating an electric current. Modern solar panels typically use silicon-based cells and can achieve efficiencies of 20 to 25 percent. Researchers are developing new materials like perovskites that could potentially achieve higher efficiencies at lower production costs.

Solar energy systems can be deployed at various scales, from small rooftop installations on individual homes to massive utility-scale solar farms covering thousands of acres. Residential solar systems allow homeowners to generate their own electricity and often sell excess power back to the grid through net metering programs. Utility-scale solar farms can generate enough electricity to power tens of thousands of homes and are becoming increasingly common in sunny regions around the world.

Energy storage is a critical challenge for solar energy because the sun does not shine consistently throughout the day or year. Battery technology, particularly lithium-ion batteries, has improved significantly and is becoming more affordable. Grid-scale battery storage systems can store excess solar energy generated during peak sunlight hours and release it when needed, helping to balance supply and demand. Some solar installations combine photovoltaic panels with thermal storage, using molten salt or other materials to store heat for later electricity generation.

The environmental benefits of solar energy are substantial. Solar power generates electricity without direct greenhouse gas emissions, helping to reduce the carbon footprint of the energy sector. Solar panels also have a relatively small land footprint compared to other energy sources, and can be installed on rooftops, parking structures, and other existing infrastructure. As technology continues to improve and costs continue to fall, solar energy is expected to play an increasingly important role in the global energy mix.
