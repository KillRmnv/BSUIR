The history of the internet begins with the development of electronic computers in the 1950s. Early computer networks were limited to connecting computers within a single organization or research institution. The ARPANET, funded by the United States Department of Defense, was one of the first wide-area networks and is considered the precursor to the modern internet. ARPANET initially connected four universities in 1969 and grew steadily throughout the 1970s, pioneering technologies like packet switching and network protocols.

The development of TCP/IP protocols in the 1970s and 1980s provided a common framework for different computer networks to communicate with each other. This standardization was crucial for the growth of the internet, as it allowed diverse networks to interconnect and share information. By the mid-1980s, the National Science Foundation's NSFNET backbone connected supercomputing centers across the United States and served as a major hub for internet traffic.

The World Wide Web, invented by Tim Berners-Lee in 1989, transformed the internet from a tool for academics and researchers into a global communication platform accessible to everyone. The web introduced concepts like hypertext, URLs, and HTML that made it easy to create and share information online. The first web browser, called WorldWideWeb, was released in 1990, and the Mosaic browser in 1993 made the web visually accessible to mainstream users. The web browser wars of the late 1990s between Netscape Navigator and Internet Explorer drove rapid innovation in web technology.

The rise of web 2.0 in the early 2000s marked a shift from static web pages to dynamic, user-generated content. Social media platforms like Facebook, Twitter, and YouTube enabled billions of people to create and share content, connect with others, and participate in global conversations. E-commerce platforms like Amazon and eBay transformed how people shop and do business. Search engines like Google made it possible to find information on any topic within seconds.

Today, the internet connects billions of devices and people worldwide, forming the backbone of modern communication, commerce, and entertainment. The Internet of Things extends internet connectivity to everyday objects, from smart home devices to industrial sensors. Cloud computing provides on-demand access to computing resources over the internet. Emerging technologies like 5G networks, edge computing, and blockchain are continuing to evolve the internet's capabilities and applications, raising new questions about privacy, security, and digital governance.
